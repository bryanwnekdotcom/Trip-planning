At a glance

The recommended route is short and realistic for a first visit: Antigua for the volcano, Lake Atitlán for swimming and scenery, then Guatemala City for Safe Passage. Semuc Champey is spectacular, but the shuttle is about 10 hours each way, so it is an alternate, not an add-on.

A $1,000 donation is generous, and Safe Passage does not publish a visit fee or a minimum donation. Ask them how they prefer to receive the gift rather than bringing a large amount of cash to campus.

If you will be under 18, plan a chaperone. Safe Passage recommends that visitors be 14 or older and that minors be accompanied by a chaperone. Confirm this when you email them. They also share safety and confidentiality guidelines before the visit; follow those while you are on campus.

Tuesday, June 22 is the better arrival if you can get it, because it gives you a full day in Antigua to acclimate before a hard volcano hike. If you land on June 23, the itinerary still works, but the Acatenango overnight should shift a day later, or you can do the easier Pacaya day hike instead.

Most itineraries from the Chicago area connect in a U.S. hub and arrive at GUA in the afternoon or evening. Once fares are in, the day-by-day can be locked to the actual arrival time. Leave generous connection time, and do not book a same-day onward shuttle that leaves before you clear immigration.

Volcano hikes

For a first trip, the Acatenango overnight is the more memorable hike: you sleep on the mountain and, if the clouds break, watch Volcán Fuego erupt at night. It is also much harder, colder, and more exposed to June rain. Pacaya is the realistic backup: a half-day walk from Antigua, lower, and officially rated easier.

Acatenango overnight

The classic overnight, with a sunrise summit at 3,976 meters and views of active Fuego if weather allows.

Reputable operators publish clear inclusions. OX: $119 plus a Q100 park fee, difficult, 6:30 a.m. start, cabins already on the mountain. Old Town Outfitters: $148-$253 depending on group size, park fees included, intermediate, 8:00 a.m. start; they are also Safe Passage's Support Team travel partner. Tropicana: from $90, park fee and 4 liters of water included, warm clothes provided.

OX leaves earlier than many companies specifically to reach camp before typical afternoon rain in the May-October wet season, which matters in June.

Pacaya day hike

Shorter and easier: Adrenalina's collective tour is $20 plus entrance, about 6 hours, with 6 a.m. and 2 p.m. departures from Antigua. The official Pacaya site lists foreigner entry at Q100 in 2026 and rates difficulty 4/10, with about 1 to 1.5 hours of ascent.

You still visit an active volcano and can walk lava fields, but you do not get the overnight Fuego show or the 3,976-meter sunrise.

Pacaya is in Escuintla department, which CDC lists among malaria-transmission departments. Discuss this at a travel clinic. The official park site says the best season is November to April, so June can be cloudy.

The U.S. State Department says not to hike trails or volcanoes without a qualified local guide. Book a named operator; do not go independently. In June, pack a rain jacket, warm layers, and boots with grip. OX warns campsite temperatures can reach about -2 C (28 F) and the summit about -5 C (23 F). Tropicana lists nighttime temperatures of 2-5 C. Start booking only after flights are set, and tell the operator you are a first-time visitor traveling solo.

Swimming and nature

Lake Atitlán is the swimming and scenery base that fits this short trip. The lake sits under volcanoes, villages are linked by public boats, and you can swim, kayak, or jump from a platform without adding a 10-hour transfer.

  • Reserva Natural Cerro Tzankujil, San Marcos La Laguna. Guatemala's cultural-information listing says it is open daily 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., with swimming areas, trails, viewpoints, and a platform about 20 meters above the lake. It does not publish an entrance fee on that page, so confirm the current price on arrival.
  • Hotel swimming access. La Casa del Mundo, in Jaibalito, is boat-access only and has lakeside swimming terraces; its FAQ says guests and staff swim there regularly and calls that part of the lake one of the cleaner stretches. La Iguana Perdida in Santa Cruz La Laguna is also on the water, with kayaking and diving as part of the usual guest scene.
  • Kayaks and village boats. Casa del Mundo recommends kayaking in the morning because afternoon wind and waves are harder for beginners, and lists public boats at about Q25, running roughly every 20 minutes between the hotel, Panajachel, and San Pedro, with last-boat times that can change. Always confirm the last return.
  • San Juan La Laguna. A short boat ride from San Pedro, known for textile cooperatives and lakeshore views.

CDC advises avoiding swimming in fresh, unchlorinated water in Guatemala because of leptospirosis, and says not to swallow the water. That includes lakes and rivers. If you do swim, use a designated area, skip it after heavy rain or if you have open cuts, and talk it through at a travel clinic.

Semuc Champey alternate

Semuc Champey's limestone pools are one of the most beautiful swimming spots in the country, but they do not fit cleanly beside both a volcano overnight and two nights at the lake. Adrenalina's Antigua-to-Lanquín shuttle is $50, leaves at 8:00 a.m., and is listed at 10 hours. The operator is blunt that these buses can be old, cramped, and delayed, and that the road is rough. A realistic Semuc add would replace the lake: shuttle on the morning after you return from the volcano, one full day at the pools, and a 10-hour ride back the next morning, arriving in time to reach Guatemala City on June 29.

A CONAP tariff sheet hosted on the agency site (uploaded November 2021) lists foreigner entry at Q50. Confirm the current fee before you go. Semuc is in Alta Verapaz, a department where CDC recommends malaria chemoprophylaxis. After heavy rain the pools and the last stretch of road can be more hazardous; ask locally the morning of the visit.

Entry requirements

For U.S. citizens, the usual tourist rules are: a valid U.S. passport, no tourist visa, and a stay of up to 90 days. The U.S. Embassy in Guatemala says U.S. citizens are not required to have a visa and are authorized entry for a 90-day tourist period. Older State Department country information, still widely cited, says the passport must be valid at the time of entry and that a visa is not required. Some airlines, and the U.K. government's advice, talk about six months of validity. Check your passport expiration and your airline's rule well before June, and allow a blank page for the entry stamp.

Guatemala requires an electronic Regional Traveler's Affidavit (Declaración Jurada Regional de Viajero) for people entering or leaving by air or sea. SAT says it can be completed up to 15 days ahead and expires on the travel date you enter. Keep the QR code from the confirmation email, on your phone and as a screenshot or printout. Complete it for the way in and again for the way out: SAT traveler declaration.

Guatemala's immigration institute also lists a return ticket, a hotel reservation or host details, and, if asked, proof of funds. Carry those even if you are not asked every time. If you are under 18 and flying alone, ask American or United about unaccompanied-minor rules; those are airline policies, not a substitute for Safe Passage's chaperone guidance. Confirm with the airline and, if needed, the Embassy of Guatemala that no extra parental letter is required for your exact situation.

Safety

As of the March 12, 2026 Travel Advisory, the State Department rates Guatemala Level 3: Reconsider Travel, because of crime and terrorism. That is the official U.S. government advice, and you should read the full page before you book. If you go, enroll in STEP, buy travel insurance that includes medical evacuation, and use a short list of hard rules.

  • Do not travel to Zone 18 in Guatemala City, the city of Villa Nueva, San Marcos Department, or Huehuetenango Department. Those are Level 4, Do Not Travel, areas on the same advisory.
  • San Marcos La Laguna on Lake Atitlán is not the same place as San Marcos Department. The lakeside village is in Sololá Department and is a normal visitor stop. The department of San Marcos is a different, restricted region in the west of the country.
  • In Guatemala City, stay in Zone 10 or 9, use Uber or a hotel taxi, and do not walk between zones after dark. Avoid showing a phone, jewelry, or a large amount of cash. The State Department also says to avoid public ATMs.
  • Safe Passage operates in a part of the city that the organization itself has described as higher risk. Go only with their plan for the day, and leave when the visit is over.
  • Do not hike without a qualified guide. Robberies can happen on trails, and help can be far away.
  • U.S. Embassy in Guatemala: Boulevard Austriaco 11-51, Zone 16, Guatemala City, +502 2354-0000. From the U.S., the State Department citizen emergency line is 1-888-407-4747. Enroll in STEP.

Health

See a travel clinic at least a month before departure, as CDC recommends. The Guatemala traveler page, last reviewed September 3, 2026, is the checklist to take to that appointment.

  • Vaccines CDC lists for most travelers include being up to date on routine shots (especially MMR), hepatitis A, and typhoid. Hepatitis B is recommended for unvaccinated travelers younger than 60. COVID-19 vaccination should be up to date. Chikungunya vaccination is generally not recommended, but mosquito bite prevention still matters because dengue and other mosquito-borne illnesses circulate.
  • Malaria: CDC says there is no malaria transmission in Antigua, Guatemala City, or at Lake Atitlán. Chemoprophylaxis is recommended for travel in Alta Verapaz, Baja Verapaz, Escuintla, Izabal, and Petén. That matters if you go to Semuc Champey (Alta Verapaz) or hike Pacaya (Escuintla). Acatenango from Antigua is not in those lake-and-city exceptions by name, so ask the clinician how they treat a Pacaya or Semuc add-on.
  • Tap water is not potable, even in the capital, according to CDC Yellow Book. Drink bottled or treated water, and be careful with ice, raw produce, and unpasteurized dairy. Typhoid vaccine is not 100% effective, so the same food and water habits still apply.
  • CDC warns about leptospirosis in fresh water and advises against swimming in lakes, ponds, or rivers. That is a real caution for Atitlán and Semuc, not a ban, and it should be part of the clinic visit.
  • Rabies is present in dogs and bats. Pre-exposure vaccine is a case-by-case decision for people who may not reach prompt care after an animal bite.

Rainy season and packing

June is in Guatemala's rainy season, generally May through October. Operators describe a common pattern of clearer mornings, clouds building by midday, and rain in the afternoon, then clearer spells later. That is why volcano views are less reliable than in the November-April dry season, why OX starts Acatenango early, why lake swimming and kayaking belong in the morning, and why Semuc can be wetter and the access road muddier. A rain day is not a spoiled trip if the schedule has slack, which this one does.
